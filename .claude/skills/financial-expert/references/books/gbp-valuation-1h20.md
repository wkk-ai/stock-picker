### p01 (aulas 1-6 of GBP Finanças Corporativas e Valuation, BTC course deck)
- Course map: ag. theory, cash cycle, budget, FCF/ROIC/EBITDA, risk/cost of capital, capital structure, EVA/MVA, DCF+multiples, PE/VC. Value = sum FCF_n/(1+WACC)^n (n to infinity); everything reduces to value maximization.
- Agency conflict example: debt R$200M, assets R$160M, risky project (20% success, R$150M return, costs R$100M) -> shareholders (equity ~0) gain from gambling while creditors lose (expected bank value 88 vs 160). App: highly levered, distressed firms (equity near option value) will look "cheap" and management may take risk; flag.
- Revenue != profit != cash. Growing firm can go bust: profitable on accrual with negative operating cash flow (US$ rev growing, OCF increasingly negative = unsustainable). App: require CFO/NI conversion check; flag NI>0 with CFO<0 persistently.
- Cash conversion cycle (CCC, "ciclo de conversão de caixa"): Dias de estoque = Estoque/(CMV/360); Dias de recebimento = Duplicatas/((Rec bruta - (devol+abat))/360); Dias fornecedores = Fornecedores/(CMV/360) (BR uses 360 days). CCC = DIE + DRC - DPF. Negative CCC (e-commerce/retail with long supplier terms or card receivables prepayment) = business model advantage; high-margin firms tolerate long cycles, low-margin must optimize. Example: 245-day CCC for electronics retailer.
- Brazil credit context: when credit dries up (post-crisis, Selic 14.25% in 2015-16) long CCC firms get squeezed; Selic high makes working-capital financing costly -> CCC and financial expense matter more. App: penalize long CCC + high net debt when Selic high.
- Quality of sales: receivables growth faster than revenue = red flag; "Rewarding sales force for growth alone", "managing to the income statement", "tying receivables to payables" listed as common cash mistakes.
- Budget types (operacional, capex, caixa); rolling, flexible, zero-based, participatory; variance analysis budget vs actual. Sensitivity example: +20% volume with -5% CMV -> +12% operating profit. Low relevance for app except sensitivity ranges.
- Itaú 2019 ROE-type question (bank benchmarking vs cost of equity) - banks judged by ROE vs Ke, not by EV multiples.
- FCF (FCFF) = EBIT x (1-t) [NOPAT] + D&A - Capex - dNWC. Capex = change in net operating long-term assets (permanentes líquidos + depreciation actually; text: Capex = LT operating assets N - N-1, using gross/permanent). dNWC from operating current assets (only operating cash, stock, receivables, prepaid; EXCLUDE financial investments) minus operating current liabilities (suppliers, taxes payable, provisions; EXCLUDE all debt).
- Worked example "Wmellon" year X: EBIT 1,206k; tax 34% (Brazil IR+CSLL statutory 34%) -> NOPAT 796k; D&A 300; capex 1,000; dNWC 610 -> FCF -514 (negative because high growth). High growth => negative FCF ok if ROIC > required return.
- ROIC = NOPAT / (NWC + operating long-term assets) = 796/4,010 = 19.86% vs investors' required 18% => value creation. Rule: ROIC > cost of capital => creates value.
- EBITDA = EBIT + D&A (also NI + IR/CSLL + fin. expense + D&A). Used to compare globally because interest/depreciation/tax rules differ. Limits: unaudited, ignores NWC need, ignores reinvestment/capex, ignores cash effect. EBITDA ~ FCF only for mature steady firms (capex ~ D&A, dNWC ~ 0, and tax ignored). App: don't treat EBITDA as cash; prefer EBITDA-Capex or FCF; flag capex-heavy firms (EV/EBITDA misleads).
- Risk/return: expected value = sum p_i x CF_i (example E(FC)=118.5); portfolio return weighted by invested weights; risk of portfolio != weighted average of risks (covariance/correlation reduces it; corr 0.67 pair cut stdev 18.5% -> 7.55%). Std dev formula over scenarios (return 17.4%, stdev 20.0% example).
- Diversification removes specific (non-systematic) risk; market risk remains (~18-20% stdev plateau after ~100+ stocks). Only systematic risk (beta) is priced.
- Beta = Cov(r_i, r_M)/Var(r_M); regression slope vs index (S&P 500 / Ibovespa). Beta caveat: low-liquidity stocks show artificially low betas (thin trading) - B3 specific. Betas Jan/2020 listed for BR firms ranged ~0.86 to 3.27. Betas change over time.
- CAPM: Ke = rf + beta x (rM - rf). Brazil example: rf = LTN (prefixed Treasury) 10%, Ibovespa return 15%, beta 1.23 => Ke 16.2%. Exercise: Restoque Ke with beta 1.08, LTN 14%, market 20%. Brazil practice in the deck: risk-free = LTN (nominal BRL), market return = historical Ibovespa average (not an ERP over US T-bond + CRP). Complexity is choosing rf and market premium. SML: project must be above SML (return vs beta) - risk-adjusted hurdle, using project beta not firm beta (A 15% beta 0.4 vs B 25% beta 1.8 vs firm 22%).
- Kd after tax = Kd nominal x (1 - T); Brazil T = 34% (example 10% -> 6.6%). Methods: average cost of existing debt (easy, but historical) or YTM of comparable-rating bonds (promised yield > expected return due to default). Restoque exercise: new debt 19% a.a. (shows Brazil cost-of-debt scale ~ Selic + spread).
- WACC = Ke x E/(D+E) + Kd x (1-T) x D/(D+E) using market values.
- Capital structure: equity cost > debt cost, but more debt raises Ke (risk conservation: total business risk constant, shifts to equity). Trade-off theory (Myers 84): optimum where marginal tax shield = marginal distress cost. Distress costs: suppliers shorten terms, clients leave, lenders demand collateral, partners stop injecting.
- Beta relevering: beta_u = beta_l / (1 + D/E x (1-T)); beta_l = beta_u x (1 + D/E (1-T)). Use comps: unlever each, average, relever at target firm's D/E. App: compare betas only after unlevering; sector D/E matters.
- ROE = NI/PL; DuPont 3-step: ROE = net margin x asset turnover (Receita/Ativos) x leverage (Ativos/PL). Margins differ by model: slide with EBITDA/net margin/ROIC for 2019 business models (e.g. EBITDA 70% vs 6%), showing high margin != high ROIC; model-dependent benchmarks.
- App implications: use ROIC vs WACC spread as quality gate; compute CCC and its trend; EBITDA vs FCF conversion check; use BRL nominal rate (LTN/Selic-linked) consistent with BRL cash flows.
### p02 (aulas 7-9: EVA/MVA, DCF, multiples, PE/VC)
- EVA = NOPAT - WACC x Capital operacional (invested capital) = (ROIC - WACC) x IC. NOPAT = EBIT x (1-t). Example: NOPAT 796k - 18% x 4,010k = +74k => value created despite FCF -514k. Positive EVA with negative FCF is typical of growth with ROIC > WACC. Levers: invest where return > WACC, raise return on existing capital, shift capital out of sub-WACC projects, tune D/E.
- MVA = market value - invested capital = PV of future EVAs (EVA/WACC for steady state + PV of projected growth EVAs). Growth alone does not create value; growth with returns above WACC does. App: score = ROIC-WACC spread x growth durability; penalize growth with ROIC<WACC.
- DCF steps: project DRE, BP, DFC -> FCF; WACC (Ke by CAPM, Kd, D/E target); terminal value. Projection drivers: Selic/interest, inflation, GDP growth, FX, seasonality, competitors; BP items by historical days (estoque, pagamento) ratios.
- Terminal value (Gordon): TV_n = FCF_n x (1+g)/(Ko - g); EV = sum PV(FCF) + PV(TV). Ko usually WACC; g=0 gives constant perpetuity. Terminal value share of total value (r=12%, FCF growth till horizon): at g=0: 57%/41%/31%/25% for 5/10/15/20-yr horizons; g=5%: 70/55/45/37%; g=10%: 90/82/75/68%. Rule: g is critical; horizon long enough for business to stabilize; TV should not be driven by horizon length. Project TV (company) is far above the <10% typical for a single project.
- Survey (AFP/HBR): practice varies widely for rf (10-yr vs 30-yr vs 90-day), beta period, ERP, horizon (5 yr most common), debt cost (current vs historical). Lesson: DCF output highly assumption-sensitive -> show ranges not point values.
- Multiples: select comparables via funnel (universe -> narrower by cost structure -> tight by region/operation); adjust for LIFO/FIFO, extraordinary and non-recurring items (asset sales), owner compensation, acquired intangibles, non-operating assets, ongoing investments, tax incentives. EV = Market cap + Debt - cash & equivalents (financial investments). EV better than market cap because includes claims of lenders. Numerators: EV for EBIT/EBITA/EBITDA/Sales; market cap for net income, dividends, cash flow, book (P/B).
- Consistency rule: numerator and denominator on the same assets (if cash excluded from EV, exclude interest income from earnings; if minorities in EV, EBIT before minorities).
- P/E example (construction sector, ResiX NI R$550M): peers P/E 5.17-15.0, mean 10.0, weighted 9.46 => valuation R$2.84-8.25bn; shows range wide; use weighted/median and peer filters.
- Best multiple = EV/EBITA (operating profit before amortization of acquired intangibles): P/E distorted by capital structure (same EV 1,000 and EBITA 100: P/E 16.7x vs 12.5x with debt; EV/EBITA same 10x); EV/EBIT distorted by acquisition amortization (7.5x vs 8.6x for same business); EV/EBITDA distorted by asset-heavy vs outsourced models (6.0x vs 8.6x while FCF equal 150, EBITA equal 300). Message: EBITDA ignores capex need. App: add EV/EBITA (or EV/(EBITDA - capex proxy)) as main operating multiple; P/E only secondary; compare peers with similar ROIC/growth.
- PE/VC: LBO targets = low debt, consistent cash flows, tangible collateral, operational inefficiency; PE good practices (HBR four disciplines): investment thesis 3-5 years, focus on a few metrics (cash and value not earnings), work the balance sheet (scarce capital, release idle WC/fixed assets, use debt for focus), shareholder-centric (sell at fair price). Applicable to listed firms: distribute excess cash, use debt to cut WACC, link pay to value. VC returns in 3-7 yrs; exits via sale or IPO. BR examples: PE investments exiting by IPO/follow-on (B3 IPOs 2018-19). App: low-debt, high-FCF, tangible-asset firms with inefficiency = classic LBO/value candidates (cheap + improvable).
- Exercise: compute Restoque EVA 2017/2018 with 2018 WACC (retailer Restoque: low quality, losses).

## Book synthesis
1. Core quality filter: ROIC = NOPAT/(NWC + operating LT assets) vs WACC; EVA = (ROIC - WACC) x IC. Cheap + ROIC>WACC is the sweet spot; cheap + ROIC<WACC is a value trap. Use BR statutory tax 34% (IR+CSLL) for NOPAT unless effective rate is better documented.
2. Valuation multiple priority for B3 and S&P: EV/EBITA (or EV/EBIT adjusted for acquisition amortization) over P/E and EV/EBITDA; P/E secondary because of leverage and non-operating results; flag EV/EBITDA for capex-heavy firms. EV = market cap + gross debt - cash and financial investments (+ minorities, preferred).
3. Cash-conversion checks: CFO/NI, FCF = NOPAT + D&A - capex - dNWC, CCC trend (days based on 360); flag profit with negative operating cash and receivables growing faster than revenue. EBITDA is not cash.
4. Brazil rate regime: Selic/CDI drives Ke and Kd; use nominal BRL rf (LTN/NTN-F, long Tesouro prefixado) with Ibovespa market return in BRL consistently with BRL cash flows; when Selic high (14.25% in 2015-16 example), long-CCC and leveraged firms are penalized and financial result dominates net income. Do not mix USD rf with BRL flows.
5. Beta handling: unlever peers' betas with D/E and 34% tax, relever at target; low-liquidity B3 stocks have artificially low betas (don't read as safe). Betas drift; allow override.
6. DCF fragility: terminal value is 55-90% of value for growth cases; cap g (<= long-run inflation + small real growth), show sensitivity table (WACC x g) instead of one number; use comps as cross-check.
7. Peer selection: sector-relative scoring after adjusting non-recurring items, LIFO/FIFO, acquired intangibles, non-operating assets; benchmarks differ by business model (EBITDA margin 70% vs 6%), so rank within sector/model.
8. Capital structure: D/E and net debt/EBITDA relative to sector, plus distress costs (suppliers shorten terms, lenders raise rates); highly levered equity near default can look cheap (agency/risk-shifting) - flag as trap. Optimal structure per trade-off theory is interior, not zero debt.
9. Growth only counts with returns above WACC (MVA = PV of EVAs): score growth quality via incremental ROIC; negative FCF is acceptable only if ROIC>WACC and funding is available.
10. PE-style screen as a bonus signal: low leverage, stable cash flow, tangible assets, below-peer margins/efficiency = upside; excess cash distribution and buybacks as shareholder-friendly signal.
