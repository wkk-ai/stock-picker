# Rosenbaum & Pearl - Investment Banking (comps, precedents, DCF focus)

### p01
- Mostly front matter (copyright, TOC, acknowledgments, intro, ValueCo sample data); little app content.
- Intro premise: comps = market benchmark, "current" valuation reflecting sentiment; can be skewed too high/low by irrational markets or market aberrations (credit crunch). DCF = intrinsic value, serves as check on market value and is essential when no pure-play peers exist. Material disconnect between methods = revisit assumptions. Implication for app: show multiple-based cheapness AND a DCF/intrinsic cross-check; flag when they disagree.
- Ch1 intro: EV-based multiples (EV/EBITDA) preferred by bankers because independent of capital structure, tax regimes, some accounting policies; P/E is most widely known but capital-structure dependent. Implication: for cross-company/cross-country (B3 vs S&P) ranking prefer EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT alongside P/E.
- Comps steps: (I) pick peers by business + financial characteristics (broad net, then "closest comparables"), (II) get data, (III) spread stats/ratios/multiples with LTM, calendarization, non-recurring adjustments, (IV) benchmark target vs peers (growth, margins, returns, credit), (V) apply multiples to target metrics. Implication: peer-relative ranking within sector/industry, on LTM and calendarized/forward numbers, using normalized (ex one-off) earnings.
- Precedent transactions: most relevant deals are last 2-3 years (similar market conditions); multiples include control premium (context only for M&A, not for minority screening).
- DCF drivers named: sales growth, margins, capex, net working capital. 
- ValueCo sample (illustrative): LTM sales 977.8, EBITDA 146.7 (15%), EBIT 127.1, capex ~2% sales, debt 300, cash 7.9; projections growth 8.1%->3% with flat 15% EBITDA margin, D&A and capex ~2% sales. Useful as unit-test fixture only.

### p02
- Ch1 steps IV-V: benchmark size, growth, margins, leverage vs peers; drop outliers, tier peers (size/sub-sector/closest vs peripheral). Valuation range: start from mean/median of peer multiple, high/low as ceiling/floor, tightest range from a few closest comps. Median preferred over mean (robust to outliers). Implication: app should rank vs sector median (not whole-market mean), exclude/winsorize outliers, show tier medians.
- Sample output page columns worth replicating: EV/Sales, EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT, P/E on LTM, current FY (NFY), NFY+1; plus % of 52-wk high, EBITDA margin, Debt/EBITDA, LT EPS growth. Sample medians (illustrative normalized market): EV/EBITDA ~7.0x, EV/EBIT ~9x, P/E ~14x LTM / 13.4x NFY / 12.5x NFY+1, EBITDA margin ~13-14%, Debt/EBITDA ~2.3x, EPS growth ~13%.
- Peer selection: same sector/sub-sector and similar size; if no direct peers broaden to businesses with same drivers (e.g., window maker -> building products/housing cycle). Business profile: sector, products, customers/end markets, distribution, geography. Financial profile: size, profitability, growth, ROI, credit. Implication: group by GICS-like sub-industry; for B3 use local peers and for S&P use US peers.
- Geography: valuation disparities by region/jurisdiction (capital market liquidity, transparency, shareholder base, political risk); bankers keep US peers separate from Europe/Asia even for same business. Even global commodity industries show geographic valuation gaps. Implication: do NOT mix B3 and S&P multiples in one peer median; compare B3 stocks to B3 peers (higher country risk/rates -> lower multiples), or adjust.
- Size: similar-size firms have similar multiples (scale, liquidity); tier e.g. <$5bn vs >$5bn equity value. Implication: size-tier peer sets; small caps trade at structurally different multiples/liquidity.
- Higher margins -> higher valuation within sector, all else equal. Growth earns higher multiples; organic growth preferred over acquisitive; mature cos: EPS growth most meaningful; early-stage/no earnings: sales or EBITDA growth. Implication: use sales/EBITDA growth when EPS is negative or tiny.
- ROI metrics: ROIC, ROE, ROA (numerator EBIT/NOPAT/NI; denominator invested capital/equity/assets); NOPAT = EBIT x (1-t). Dividend yield is also a return metric. Credit profile: leverage + coverage + agency ratings.
- % of 52-week high = price / 52wk high; if far out of line with peers it flags company-specific problem (missed guidance, management/operations issues), not sector-wide. Implication: a stock cheap AND far below its 52wk high vs peers = possible value trap flag or opportunity; check cause.
- Forward vs LTM: comps use both; forward estimates more meaningful depending on sector/cycle; M&A/debt contexts emphasise LTM. Use one consensus source consistently; screen estimates for stale/outlier ones (e.g., after transformative acquisition). Implication: filter stale analyst estimates; be consistent in data source.
- Data sources: price from prior day close; share count from latest 10-K/10-Q cover or proxy; options from 10-K; EBITDA is non-GAAP, not on statements (compute EBIT + D&A from cash flow statement); credit ratings from agency sites.
- Equity value = share price x fully diluted shares. FDSO = basic + net new shares from ITM options/warrants (treasury stock method) + shares from ITM converts. TSM: proceeds = options x strike; repurchased = proceeds / price; net new = options - repurchased. Example: 5.0m options, strike 18, price 20 -> 4.5m repurchased, net 0.5m, FDSO 100.5m. Conservative practice uses all outstanding ITM options, not only exercisable.
- Convertibles: if ITM (price > conversion price) treat as equity via if-converted method (or net share settlement) and exclude from debt; if OTM treat as debt. Implication: avoid double counting converts in both debt and shares; B3 data usually lacks this, but handle ADR/BDR and preferred.
- Input template items: LTM, adjusted (non-recurring stripped) gross profit, EBIT, EBITDA, net income, adjusted diluted EPS; marginal tax rate; predicted beta; credit stats (Debt/Cap, Debt/EBITDA, Net debt/EBITDA, EBITDA/interest, (EBITDA-capex)/interest, EBIT/interest).

### p03
- Convertibles (if-converted): ITM convert -> new shares = amount outstanding / conversion price (e.g. $150m / $15 = 10m); treat as equity, remove from debt; add back after-tax coupon to net income (EPS usually still diluted). Net share settlement: only excess over par converts to shares = (shares x price - par) / price (10m x $20 = 200; minus 150 = 50; /20 = 2.5m shares); par stays as debt.
- EV = fully diluted equity value + total debt + preferred stock + noncontrolling interest - cash & equivalents. EV is capital-structure neutral (raising debt held as cash, or equity to repay debt, leaves EV unchanged). Exception: highly leveraged companies often trade at a discount (distress risk, growth constraints). Implication: include preferred and minorities in EV; flag heavy leverage as discount that may be a trap rather than bargain.
- Sales: scale/market share/purchasing power earns premium vs smaller peers. Gross margin = (Sales-COGS)/Sales, gauge of pricing power/efficiency. EBITDA = EBIT + D&A (D&A from cash-flow statement; COGS may include or exclude D&A). EBITDA is proxy for operating cash flow, free of capital structure and tax; EBIT better when D&A differences/high capex matter but distorted by depreciation policy and acquisition amortization.
- Net income margin affected by leverage and tax rate; two firms with same operating margin can differ.
- Growth: CAGR = (End/Begin)^(1/years) - 1 (e.g. EPS 1.00->1.30 over 2 yrs = 14.0%). Adjust historical EPS for non-recurring items. Long-term growth = analyst 3-5 yr EPS growth estimate.
- ROIC = EBIT / average (net debt + equity) [or NOPAT]; ROE = NI / avg equity; ROA = NI / avg total assets; use averages of opening and closing balances. Dividend yield = latest quarterly DPS x 4 / price.
- Leverage: Debt/EBITDA (also net debt/EBITDA, senior secured/EBITDA, debt/(EBITDA-capex)), read as years of cash flow to repay debt; Debt/Total Cap = Debt / (Debt + preferred + NCI + equity), book or market. Coverage: EBITDA/interest, (EBITDA-capex)/interest, EBIT/interest; higher is better. Ratings scale: Baa3/BBB- is lowest investment grade; Ba1/BB+ and below speculative; B- Caa highly speculative/substantial risk. Implication: map debt ratios to safety flags; use (EBITDA-capex)/interest for capex-heavy firms.
- LTM = last FY + current YTD - prior-year YTD. If latest period is Q4, FY = LTM. Use earnings releases to update before 10-Q.
- Calendarization: CY sales = (Month# x FY_A + (12-Month#) x FY_next)/12 where Month# = fiscal-year-end month (e.g. April FYE: 4/12 x FY08A + 8/12 x FY09E; 1000 and 1150 -> 1100). Implication: needed if fiscal years differ (B3 mostly Dec; some S&P non-Dec FYE e.g. retailers, tech).
- Non-recurring scrubbing: add back restructuring, asset-sale losses, impairments, inventory write-offs, debt extinguishment, litigation; remove gains from asset sales, favorable settlements, tax benefits. Search filings for "non-recurring/unusual/one-time". Judgment: recurring-in-nature items (e.g. generic pharma litigation) should not be stripped. Pre-tax add-back to EBIT/EBITDA at full amount; for net income tax-effect at marginal rate (example: $15m charges, 40% tax, +$9m NI; EPS 2.00 -> 2.30; EBIT 135->150, EBITDA 185->200). After-tax disclosed amounts gross up by /(1-t) for EBIT. Marginal US rate ~35% federal + 2-5% state (book-era). Implication: use adjusted EBIT/EPS for multiples; apply Brazil 34% (IRPJ/CSLL) and US 21%+state for current gross-ups.
- Recent events (M&A, financing, splits, buybacks, convert conversions) require pro forma adjustment of balance sheet and income before computing multiples; confirm consensus estimates already updated.
- P/E = price / diluted EPS = equity value / net income. Forward EPS mainly (next two calendar years), LTM secondary. Higher P/E = higher growth expectations. Limitations: meaningless when earnings ~0/negative; depends on leverage, tax, depreciation accounting. Best for mature, steady earners.
- EV/EBITDA = valuation standard for most sectors (neutral to capital structure, taxes, D&A). EV/EBIT less common but useful for high-capex firms or when D&A unavailable. EV/Sales only a sanity check, or for early-stage no-earnings firms. Implication: gate P/E when EPS <= 0 and fall back to EV/EBITDA or EV/Sales.
- Sector-specific multiples (Exh 1.33): EV/EBITDAR for casinos, restaurants, retail (lease-adjusted); EV/EBITDAX for oil & gas exploration/natural resources; EV/subscriber or POP, access lines for telecom/media; EV/reserves, EV/capacity for mining/oil/paper; EV/sq ft for real estate/retail; P/Book for banks (financial institutions) and homebuilders; FFO per share and cash available for distribution for REITs/real estate; NAV per share for financials/real estate; discretionary cash flow for natural resources. Implication: banks use P/B (P/E, ROE), REITs use P/FFO, not EV/EBITDA.
- Benchmarking: compare financials AND understand each peer's story (market share gainer/loser, delivery vs guidance, M&A, management change). Tier peers; outliers excluded; best 2-3 comps drive the range, tighter than universe high-low. Period choice (LTM, 1-yr or 2-yr forward) depends on sector, cycle, trust in consensus.
- Implied valuation math: implied EV = multiple x metric; equity = EV - net debt; price = equity / FDSO. Example 2008E EBITDA 215 x 6.0-7.0x = EV 1,290-1,505; net debt 500 -> equity 790-1,005; /100m sh = 7.90-10.05. P/E route: 11-14x x NI 75 = equity 825-1,050; EV = equity + net debt. Note FDSO varies with price under TSM (circular).
- Final check: cross-check comps range against precedents, DCF, LBO. Implication: composite "cheapness" should triangulate relative (peer multiples) and absolute (DCF/earnings yield) rather than rely on one.

### p04
- Cross-check rule: big gaps between methods signal wrong assumptions/math. Common comps errors: bad/over-weighted peers, wrong FDSO/EV/LTM/calendarization, failing to scrub non-recurring items and recent events. Implication: unit-test these in the app (FDSO via TSM, EV bridge, LTM, adjusted EBIT).
- Comps pros: market-based, relative, quick, current. Cons: skewed in exuberance/panic, no pure-play peers, disconnect from cash flow (DCF), misses company-specific strengths/risks. Implication: market multiples can be systematically cheap in bear markets; pair with absolute value check.
- Peer-finding sources worth mimicking: competitors named in 10-K, analyst reports, merger-proxy fairness opinions, SIC/NAICS screens. Implication: app can define peers via sector/industry code + size band.
- Worked example (Momper, price 20.00, 80% of 52wk high 25.00): basic shares 48.5m; options tranches 1.25m@5, 1.0m@10, 0.5m@17.5 ITM, 0.25m@30 OTM; proceeds 25.0 -> 1.25m repurchased -> net 1.5m -> FDSO 50.0m; equity 1,000; debt 550; cash 50; EV 1,500 (net debt 500).
- Adjusted LTM: reported EBIT 165 + 8 inventory charge + 12 restructuring - 10 prior-year gain = adj EBIT 175 (2007 gain removed, current-year charges added); D&A 40 -> adj EBITDA 215 (15.2% margin). Adj NI = 78.8 + (8+12-10... net) with tax adj at 38% -> 85.0; adj EPS 1.70 vs reported 1.56. Multiples on adjusted: EV/EBITDA 7.0x LTM, 6.7x 2008E, 6.3x 2009E; EV/EBIT 8.6x; P/E 11.8x LTM, 11.1x, 10.0x. Reported vs adjusted P/E: 12.8x vs 11.8x - one-offs materially shift the multiple.
- Ratios as computed: ROIC = adj LTM EBIT 175 / avg(net debt + equity) = 15.8%; ROE = 85 / avg equity (565, 600) = 14.6%; ROA = 85 / avg assets = 6.1%; div yield = 0.10x4/20 = 2.0%; Debt/Cap = 550/(550+600) = 47.8% (book); Debt/EBITDA 2.6x, net 2.3x; EBITDA/interest 5.7x; (EBITDA-capex)/interest 4.9x; EBIT/interest 4.6x. LT EPS growth 15% (analyst).
- Growth examples: sales 1-yr = 2008E/2007A - 1; 2-yr CAGR = (2009E/2007A)^(1/2)-1 using adjusted base (EBITDA 240/200 -> 9.5%). Historical growth must be on adjusted figures.
- Peer sample statistics (illustrative): peer EV/EBITDA mostly 6.7-7.5x; P/E 12-15x; EBITDA margin 12-17%; Debt/EBITDA 1-3.8x; ROIC 8-23%; credit BB to A-. Premium valuation tracks: higher margin/growth/ROIC/credit -> higher multiple (e.g., Vucic 20% margin 8.5x vs Paris 10% margin 5.5x). Implication: cheapness score should be margin/growth/quality-adjusted - a low multiple on a low-margin, high-leverage, small, low-ROIC name is explained, not a bargain.
- Outliers (e.g. largest and smallest, with divergent size/profitability) were kept but tiered (large/mid/small) rather than deleted; closer tiers count more. Closest comps = similar size, margin, leverage.
- Selected range logic: sector trades on forward EV/EBITDA; picked 6.25-7.25x 2008E, 5.75-6.75x 2009E, 6.5-7.5x LTM from closest comps (peer overall median 7.0x/6.7x/6.3x); ValueCo EV range ~ $932-1,100m (EBITDA 146.7/150/162). Football field = bar chart of ranges per method for comparison. Implication: output range/bar per method.
- Ch2 start: precedent multiples (transaction comps) normally exceed trading comps because of control premium and synergies. Recent 2-3 years most relevant unless same cycle point. Universe via M&A databases, target/peer M&A history, merger proxy fairness opinions, research. Public-target deal data easy (SEC); private deals often incomplete. Implication: takeover-style fair value for B3/S&P screening should not use precedent multiples as minority trading fair value; they overstate.

### p05
- Precedent transaction multiples (illustrative sample 10 deals, 2006-08): EV/LTM EBITDA mean/median 7.5x (range 6.5-8.5x), EV/EBIT ~9x (8.1-12.5x), EV/Sales 1.2x, P/E 13.4-13.7x (10.6-17.7x), premiums to unaffected price ~33-36% (range 27-43%). Compare trading comps in same sample ~7.0x EBITDA, ~14x P/E - so deals ~0.5x higher on EV/EBITDA in this illustration. Implication: typical takeover premium 30-35% is a rough upper bound for "full value"; can be used as a sanity check on upside.
- Precedent multiples use actual LTM at announcement (not projections), adjusted for non-recurring items and recent events; buyers' projections are not public and buyers do not give full credit anyway.
- Context that changes multiples: market conditions (credit availability: cheap debt in mid-2000s allowed higher prices; 2007-08 credit crunch cut them; tech bubble multiples became irrelevant afterward). Implication: multiples are regime dependent; do not mix cycle peaks and troughs in a fair-value anchor (use own-history ranges with cycle awareness; relevant to Brazil's Selic regime).
- Deal dynamics: strategic buyers usually pay more than sponsors (synergies, lower cost of capital), except when credit abundant; scarcity value; seller motivations (forced divestiture -> lower price); auctions and hostile contests raise price; merger of equals forgoes premium; stock consideration yields lower premium than all-cash.
- Equity value for public target = offer price x fully diluted shares at offer price (TSM at offer price; all ITM options count whether vested or not because vesting accelerates on change of control). If less than 100% acquired, gross up to 100%. Private target: equity = EV - net debt assumed/refinanced. EV = equity + net debt + preferred + NCI (same bridge as comps).
- Exchange ratio = offer price / acquirer price; fixed exchange ratio (value floats with acquirer price), floating ratio (value fixed, shares float; used when acquirer much bigger), collar guarantees range (e.g. $18-22). Cash+stock: offer = cash + ratio x acquirer price. Offer-price example: 0.5 x $40 = $20; 50m shares x $20 = $1,000m.
- Premium paid = Offer / Unaffected price - 1 (e.g. 20/16 -> 25%); measure vs 1 day, 7 days, 30 days (also 60/90/180-day averages, 52wk high/low) prior; if leaks/"strategic alternatives" announced, use earlier unaffected date. Implication: when price already jumped on rumors, premium base is not unaffected.
- Synergies: strategic buyers; announced synergy estimates help explain high multiples; multiples normally shown without synergy adjustment, adjusted multiples (EV / (EBITDA + synergies)) used for deeper understanding.
- Filings sources for deals (proxy PREM14A/DEFM14A, Schedule TO/14D-9, S-4/424B, 13E-3, 8-K) - not important for app, except for Brazil analogs (CVM fatos relevantes, OPA documents). Private-private deals mostly opaque -> unusable.
- Sector-specific deal multiples same as Ch1 list (P/B for banks etc.).

### p06
- Synergy-adjusted multiple: EV/(LTM EBITDA + run-rate cost synergies, excl. costs to achieve); e.g. 1,200/150 = 8.0x vs 1,200/(150+30) = 6.7x.
- Precedents benchmarking: recent deal, direct competitor, similar profile = most relevant; peak-credit LBOs via auction less relevant later. Valuation: use mean/median as preliminary, anchor on 2-3 best deals (e.g., mean 7.0x but best deals 7.5-8.0x -> pick 7.0-8.0x). Key multiples: EV/LTM EBITDA and P/E (offer price / LTM EPS).
- Red flag: precedent range far BELOW trading-comps range -> recheck universes/calcs; may be legit if sector is in play or at cyclical high. Implication: when market multiple > takeover multiple, sector is at a cyclical high/expensive.
- Precedents pros: market-based, current, relative, simple, objective (no projections). Cons: skewed by capital markets, time lag, few comps, info gaps, buyer's basis is private forward expectations.
- Screening in the example: last 3 years, EV $250m-$3,000m, sector; eliminate size/business mismatches. Rosenbaum deal: bidding contest ($18 sponsor vs $20 strategic) explains above-mean multiple (8.0x). Premium: 15% vs last close but 27-33% vs unaffected price (before strategic-alternatives announcement); typical public M&A premium ~30%. Litigation settlement $15m added back (adj EBITDA 250 vs 235 reported); tax-effect 38% -> NI 125 vs 115.7. Adj multiples: EV/EBITDA 8.0x, EV/EBIT 9.1x, P/E 13.6x, EV/Sales 1.5x.
- ValueCo result: precedent 7.0-8.0x LTM EBITDA 146.7 = EV $1,027-1,173m, slightly above comps 6.5-7.5x (premium from control); football field overlay of methods.
- Ch3 DCF intro: value = PV of projected unlevered FCF + PV of terminal value; DCF = intrinsic value; check on market value; useful with no pure-play peers; project typically 5 yrs (longer if cyclical/immature), long enough to span a cycle and reach steady state. Terminal-year numbers must be normalized (not a cyclical peak or trough). Discount at WACC. Output is a range via sensitivity (WACC, exit multiple or perpetuity growth, growth, margins), not a point. DCF strength: insulated from market distortions, scenario flexibility; weakness: only as good as assumptions.
- Unlevered FCF = EBIT x (1-t) [EBIAT/NOPAT] + D&A - capex - increase in NWC. Example (ValueCo 2009E): EBIT 140.4, tax 53.4 (38% approx), EBIAT 87.0, +D&A 21.6, -capex 21.6, -dNWC 6.5 = FCF 85.8 (D&A = capex steady-state).
- Discounting: discount factor = 1/(1+WACC)^t. Mid-year convention used: periods 0.5, 1.5, ..., 4.5 (factor 0.95, 0.86, 0.77, 0.69, 0.63 at 11%); terminal value discounted at end-year factor (0.59 = 1/1.11^5 with exit multiple method, i.e., period 5.0; the 2013 TV is discounted at N=5 per output).
- Example output: WACC 11%, exit multiple 7.0x on 2013E EBITDA 189.5 -> TV 1,326.3; PV of TV 787.1 = 69.4% of EV; PV of FCFs 346.3; EV 1,133.3; implied EV/LTM EBITDA 7.7x; implied perpetuity growth 3.0%; equity = EV - debt 300 + cash 25 = 858.3; /50.0 FDSO = $17.17. Sensitivity grid: WACC 10-12% x multiple 6-8x gives EV 984-1,295; implied perpetual growth 0.9-4.8%. Implication: terminal value ~70% of DCF value -> show TV share and implied growth as sanity checks; flag if implied g > long-run nominal GDP.
- DCF must tie to comps/precedents/LBO; large disconnect signals assumption problems.

### p07
- FCF (unlevered) = EBIT - taxes at marginal rate (= EBIAT/NOPAT) + D&A - capex - increase in NWC; independent of capital structure (cash to debt and equity holders). Use 3-year history, normalized for non-recurring items, as base for assumptions (reliable for mature non-cyclical firms).
- Projection horizon: 5 yrs usually (spans at least a cycle); 10+ yrs for early-stage high growth or contracted-revenue sectors (natural resources, satellite, utilities). Build management / base / upside / downside cases with a scenario toggle. Implication: scenario cases for the app's fair-value (bear/base/bull).
- Sales: first 2-3 years from consensus; step growth down gradually to a long-term 2-4% by terminal year; cyclicals: peak/trough early then revert to normalized terminal-year level (otherwise terminal value, often large, is skewed). Sanity-check vs history, peers and sector; growth must be consistent with capex and NWC assumptions (higher growth needs more capex/WC). Margins: hold last consensus margin constant unless justified; operating leverage.
- Tax: marginal 35-40% (US era) vs effective rate as reference; effective differs due to credits, non-deductible items, valuation allowances; GAAP D&A used as proxy for tax D&A. Implication: Brazil 34% statutory (IRPJ+CSLL); watch JCP (interest on equity) shield.
- D&A: non-cash, add back; project as % of sales or capex, or PP&E schedule (PP&E_t = PP&E_{t-1} + capex - depreciation); by the terminal year set D&A about equal to capex so PP&E base is steady (growth-consistent). Goodwill not amortized, tested for impairment. Capex is cash outflow; project as % of sales per history unless expansion or harvest mode; planned capex in MD&A. Implication: maintenance capex vs growth capex distinction; D&A ~ capex steady state check; FCF yield should use capex properly.
- NWC = (A/R + inventory + prepaid & other CA) - (A/P + accrued + other CL); increase = use of cash. Ratios: DSO = A/R / Sales x 365; DIH = Inventory / COGS x 365; inventory turns = COGS / Inventory; DPO = A/P / COGS x 365. Prepaids/accruals as % of sales. Hold ratios constant at last year or 3-yr avg. Rising DSO/DIH = cash-tied-up red flag (customer credit, obsolescence); high DPO helps liquidity "within reason". Implication: quality flags: DSO/DIH drift up, receivables growing faster than sales.
- WACC = Kd(1-t) x D/(D+E) + Ke x E/(D+E), market-value weights, target capital structure (use company's current if within peer range, else peer mean/median; private: peer median). WACC falls with debt until optimal structure, then distress costs raise it. Sum-of-the-parts DCF with separate WACC for diverse segments (holdings/conglomerates).
- Cost of debt: current market yield on outstanding bonds (yield-to-worst for callables) or from credit-rating comps; coupons at issuance are backward-looking; tax-effect it.
- Cost of equity CAPM: Ke = rf + beta_L x (rm - rf) [+ size premium]. rf: 10-yr (or longer / 20-yr interpolated) government yield, match duration to going concern. Market risk premium: Wall Street 4-8%; Ibbotson 1926-2007 historical arithmetic 7.1%; firms set a house number. Beta: S&P 500 =1.0; prefer forward-looking predicted (Barra) over 2-5 yr historical. Unlever: beta_U = beta_L / (1 + (D/E)(1-t)); average peers; relever: beta_L = beta_U x (1 + (D/E)(1-t)) at target D/E. Size premium (Ibbotson deciles by market cap) added for small caps because beta understates risk (thin trading). Implication: for B3 use BRL rf (NTN-B/ DI long) and Brazil ERP/country risk; for S&P ~4-5% ERP; small caps need size premium.
- Terminal value = typically 3/4 or more of DCF value. Exit multiple method: TV = EBITDA_n x multiple (use normalized, not peak/trough, multiple and EBITDA; sensitize 6.0-8.0x around 6.5-7.5x). Perpetuity growth: TV = FCF_n x (1+g)/(r-g); g typically 2-4% (about nominal GDP). Cross-check: implied g = (TV x WACC - FCF)/(TV + FCF) for end-year; mid-year version uses FCF x (1+WACC)^0.5; implied exit multiple from PGM = TV/EBITDA_n (mid-year: TV x (1+WACC)^0.5 / EBITDA_n). If implied g is too high/low or implied multiple differs from normalized peers, revisit assumptions.
- PV: discount factor = 1/(1+WACC)^n; 10%, one year -> 0.91.
- Implication for app: simple Gordon/residual-income fair value must sanity check g<=nominal GDP, WACC>g, and terminal share of value; BRL nominal growth ~ inflation+real growth, so g assumptions differ between B3 and S&P.

### p08
- Mid-year convention: discount factor = 1/(1+WACC)^(n-0.5) (10%: 0.95 vs year-end 0.91 for year 1); gives slightly higher value; not suited to highly seasonal businesses. PGM terminal value also mid-year discounted; exit-multiple TV uses year-end (n=full years) because multiple is on year-end LTM EBITDA. EV = sum FCF_t/(1+W)^(t-0.5) + EBITDA_n x multiple/(1+W)^n.
- Equity value = EV - debt - preferred - noncontrolling interest + cash; per share = equity / FDSO, which is circular with option dilution under TSM (iterate: ITM options depend on implied price). Example EV 1,000 - debt 250 - NCI 25 + cash 50 = 775; 50m basic + net 1.667m options -> 51.667m; $15.00/share.
- Sensitivity: WACC x exit multiple (and implied perpetuity growth, implied EV/LTM EBITDA, PV of TV % of EV) plus growth x margin tables. ValueCo: +50bp growth and +50bp EBIT margin adds $38m EV (~3.4%). DCF range chosen from WACC 10.5-11.5% x exit 6.5-7.5x = EV $1,057-1,213m (above comps $932-1,100 and in line with precedents $1,027-1,173). Implication: a +/-1pt WACC change moves EV roughly +/-8%; show sensitivity bands, not point estimates. TV share stays 66-73% across the grid.
- Common DCF missteps: unrealistic projections (largest impact), WACC, terminal value. Using unchallenged management case is a pitfall. Cons: forecast dependence, sensitivity, TV 3/4+ of value, constant capital structure.

### p09
- Ch4 LBO fundamentals (participants, candidate traits, economics). Mostly deal-process; app-relevant bits only.

- Exit routes: strategic sale (highest price, synergies), sponsor sale, IPO (partial exit), dividend recap. Multiple expansion drivers: scale, op improvements, shift to higher-valued segments, faster growth, cycle timing.
- Rest (fund structure 1-2% fee/20% carry, bank meetings, roadshow, commitment letters) not relevant.

## Book synthesis

### (a) Comparable-companies method, step by step (Ch1, p01-p04)
1. Peer selection: start broad then narrow. Business profile = sector/sub-sector (SIC, NAICS, GICS-like), products, customers/end markets, distribution, geography. Financial profile = size (market cap tiers, e.g. <$5bn vs >$5bn), profitability, growth, ROIC, credit. Sources: competitors named in 10-K, analyst reports, fairness-opinion peer lists, screens. If no direct peers, broaden to businesses with the same drivers. Keep regions apart (US vs Europe/Asia; here B3 vs S&P 500) because of structural valuation gaps (liquidity, rates, political risk).
2. Collect data: price (prior close), basic shares (latest cover page), options/warrants/converts (10-K), debt, preferred, noncontrolling interest, cash; LTM and consensus estimates (use one source consistently, drop stale ones).
3. Spread (normalize):
   - FDSO via treasury stock method: net new shares = options - options x strike/price (ITM only); ITM convertibles via if-converted (shares = amount / conversion price, remove from debt, add back after-tax coupon) or net share settlement.
   - Equity value = price x FDSO. EV = equity + total debt + preferred + NCI - cash.
   - LTM = last FY + current YTD - prior YTD. Calendarize non-Dec fiscal years: CY = (m x FY_a + (12-m) x FY_b)/12, m = fiscal-year-end month.
   - Strip non-recurring items: add back restructuring, impairments, write-offs, litigation, debt extinguishment losses; remove asset-sale gains, tax benefits. Add to EBIT/EBITDA pre-tax, to net income after tax at marginal rate. Do not strip items recurring in nature. Adjust for recent events (M&A, buybacks, financings) pro forma.
   - Metrics: sales, gross profit, EBITDA (EBIT + D&A), EBIT, net income, diluted EPS; margins; growth (CAGR on adjusted base); ROIC = EBIT / avg(net debt + equity); ROE, ROA on averages; dividend yield; Debt/EBITDA, net debt/EBITDA, Debt/Cap, EBITDA/interest, (EBITDA-capex)/interest, EBIT/interest; % of 52-wk high.
4. Multiples per sector: EV/EBITDA as default standard; EV/EBIT for high-capex or D&A-distorted names; P/E (forward) for mature steady earners (not when EPS <= 0); EV/Sales only as sanity check or for no-earnings firms. Sector specifics: EV/EBITDAR (retail, restaurants, casinos), EV/EBITDAX (oil and gas), EV/subscriber/POP (telecom/media), EV/reserves or capacity (mining, oil, paper), EV/sq ft (real estate/retail), P/Book (banks, financials, homebuilders), P/FFO and cash available for distribution (REITs), NAV per share (real estate/financials).
5. Periods: LTM, current year, next year (and +1); forward preferred in cyclical/growth sectors, LTM in M&A/credit contexts.
6. Benchmark and tier: compare size, growth, margins, ROIC, leverage, credit; tier peers (closest vs peripheral); keep outliers but tier them or exclude with reason; premium multiples track higher margin, growth, ROIC, credit, size. Understand each peer's story (guidance misses, M&A, management change).
7. Statistics: max, 75th percentile, mean, median, 25th, min for each multiple. Median preferred over mean (outlier robust).
8. Value range: begin from peer median, then judgment-select a range (typically about +/-0.5x around the closest-comps center, e.g. 6.5-7.5x LTM, 6.25-7.25x on current year, 5.75-6.75x next year) from the 2-3 closest comps; high/low of the universe are the ceiling/floor, not the answer. Implied EV = multiple x target metric; equity = EV - net debt (+ preferred, NCI); price = equity / FDSO (circular under TSM, iterate). Cross-check with precedents, DCF, LBO on a football field.

### (b) DCF essentials and sanity checks (Ch3, p06-p08)
- UFCF = EBIT x (1-t) + D&A - capex - increase in NWC. Projection 5 yrs (10+ for early-stage or contracted-revenue); sales: consensus first 2-3 yrs, fade to long-term 2-4% by terminal year; cyclicals normalize terminal year; margins flat unless justified; capex = D&A in steady state; NWC ratios (DSO, DIH, DPO, % of sales) held at last year or 3-yr average. Build management/base/upside/downside cases.
- WACC = Kd(1-t) x D/(D+E) + Ke x E/(D+E), market-value weights at target capital structure (current if within peer range, else peer mean/median). Ke = rf + beta_L x MRP (+ size premium); rf = 10-20y government yield; MRP 4-8% (7.1% historical Ibbotson arithmetic in book); unlever peer betas beta_U = beta_L/(1+(D/E)(1-t)), average, relever at target D/E; prefer forward (predicted) betas; size premium (~1.65% for smallest decile) for small caps; Kd = current market yield on debt (YTW), tax-effected. Worked example: 4% + 1.20 x 7.1% + 1.65% = 14.1% Ke; Kd 3.7% after tax; 30% debt -> WACC 11.0%; range +/-0.5pt.
- Terminal value: exit multiple method TV = EBITDA_n x normalized multiple (not peak or trough; sensitize +/-0.5-1.0x); perpetuity growth TV = FCF_n(1+g)/(WACC-g) with g 2-4% (about nominal GDP, never above long-run nominal growth). Cross-check each with the other: implied g = (TV x WACC - FCF)/(TV + FCF) (end-year); implied multiple = TV/EBITDA_n (mid-year: TV x (1+WACC)^0.5 / EBITDA). Mid-year convention: factor 1/(1+WACC)^(n-0.5) for flows; exit-multiple TV still discounted at full n.
- Sanity checks: TV share of EV (typically ~70%, "3/4 or more"; flag higher); implied g in 2-4%; implied EV/EBITDA near peer range; WACC > g; terminal D&A about capex; sensitivity grids WACC x exit multiple/growth (+/-1pt WACC about +/-8% EV; +50bp growth and +50bp margin about +3.4% EV). Output = range (e.g. WACC 10.5-11.5% x 6.5-7.5x), never a point; reconcile with comps/precedents/LBO; large gaps mean broken assumptions. Equity = EV - debt - preferred - NCI + cash; per share on FDSO.
- Pitfalls: unchallenged management projections (biggest), WACC errors, TV dominance, constant capital structure.

### (c) Credit/leverage stats and thresholds (Ch1, Ch4-5, p03, p09-p12)
- Definitions: Debt/EBITDA (years of EBITDA to repay), net debt/EBITDA, senior secured/EBITDA, debt/(EBITDA-capex), Debt/Total Cap = Debt/(Debt + preferred + NCI + equity) book or market, EBITDA/interest, (EBITDA-capex)/interest, EBIT/interest, fixed charge coverage = cash flow/(interest + preferred dividends + rent).
- Ratings: Baa3/BBB- lowest investment grade; Ba1/BB+ and below speculative (LBO debt); B/Caa highly speculative.
- Benchmarks from the book: average LBO total leverage 3.4x-4.6x in normal years, 5.2-6.1x at 2006-07 peak, 5.0x in 2008; equity 30-40% of financing (39% post-crunch); typical LBO at close ~5x total, ~3x senior, debt/cap ~67%, EBITDA/interest ~2.4-2.5x, (EBITDA-capex)/interest ~2.1-2.2x; healthy deleveraging trajectory to ~1.5x total, ~26% debt/cap, EBITDA/interest ~6x within 7 years; banks want bank debt repaid within tenor (5-7 yrs) from cumulative FCF, else refinancing risk. Sample public-peer universe: Debt/EBITDA 1.0-3.8x, median ~2.3x, EBITDA/interest ~5-6x, ratings BB to A-.
- Covenant style thresholds: maintenance covenants (max total/senior leverage, min interest coverage) step down over time; ABL springing FCCR 1.0x. Leverage raises equity returns (25% debt IRR 14.9% vs 75% debt IRR 28.3% at same EV) but lifts distress risk; highly leveraged companies often trade at a discount.
- Structural: HoldCo debt is structurally junior to OpCo debt; secured and senior rank first; floating-rate debt makes coverage rate-sensitive.
- LBO value = floor: valuation at 20% IRR over 5 yrs with 25-35% equity usually sits at the low end of the football field; implied floor multiple = debt multiple/(1 - min equity %) (4.5x/0.75 = 6.0x).

### (d) Top 10 implications for the app
1. Rank within peer group, not the whole market: use sub-industry plus size-tier peer medians (median, not mean), winsorize or exclude outliers, and never mix B3 and S&P 500 multiples in one peer set (country risk, rates, liquidity differ); compare B3 names with B3 peers.
2. Default multiple set: EV/EBITDA and EV/EBIT as primary cross-company measures (capital-structure neutral), forward P/E for mature earners, EV/Sales only as fallback; auto-fall back when EPS or EBITDA <= 0. Banks/insurers: P/B and ROE; REITs: P/FFO; E&P: EV/EBITDAX; retail/airlines: EV/EBITDAR and lease-inclusive debt; real estate: NAV.
3. Build the EV bridge correctly: FDSO by treasury stock method, converts if-converted (no double counting), plus debt, preferred, NCI minus cash; include lease liabilities consistently; unit-test these.
4. Normalize earnings before computing any multiple: strip non-recurring items (tax-effect at 34% Brazil, ~21% plus state US), use LTM and calendarized figures, pro forma for M&A/buybacks. Show reported vs adjusted multiple; large gaps are a flag (the book's example moved P/E 12.8x to 11.8x).
5. Quality-adjust cheapness: a low multiple on low margin, low ROIC, high leverage, small size is explained, not a bargain. Score margin, growth, ROIC (EBIT / avg(net debt + equity)), and credit alongside the multiple; use % of 52-week high versus peers to flag company-specific problems (possible value trap or opportunity; check the cause).
6. Triangulate: show a football field of ranges (peer-multiple range, own-history range, simple DCF range, LBO/earnings-yield floor) and flag disagreement between methods instead of a single point estimate.
7. Simple DCF defaults: growth fading to 2-4% (nominal-GDP-like; use higher nominal g for BRL, lower for USD), flat margins, capex ~ D&A at steady state, WC days flat, WACC from CAPM with BRL rf (NTN-B/DI long) and Brazil ERP or S&P ERP 4-5%, small-cap size premium; always display TV share of EV (flag > ~75-80%), implied g (flag > nominal GDP), implied exit multiple vs peers, and a WACC x g/multiple sensitivity grid (+/-1pt WACC about +/-8% value).
8. Leverage and coverage flags: Net debt/EBITDA (comfortable < ~3x, caution 3-5x, LBO-like/stress > 5x), EBITDA/interest (<2.5x weak, >5x comfortable), (EBITDA-capex)/interest for capex-heavy names, Debt/Cap, rating bucket; check years-to-repay = net debt / levered FCF (<= 5-7 yrs). In high-Selic Brazil, interest coverage and floating (CDI) exposure matter more than P/E alone; high leverage inflates ROE/EPS growth, so use DuPont or ROIC.
9. Cash-flow quality checks from the NWC section: DSO/DIH drifting up, receivables growing faster than sales, DPO stretched, capex consistently below D&A (harvesting), growth with weak FCF. Use levered FCF (after interest) for equity FCF yield and unlevered FCF for EV/FCF; do not mix.
10. Regime awareness and takeover context: multiples depend on credit/market cycle (credit boom vs crunch; Selic regime), so anchor to a peer/own-history range with cycle awareness, not a single historical mean; precedent/takeover multiples include a ~30-35% control premium (7.5x vs 7.0x trading in the book's sample) and should be an upside sanity bound, not minority fair value; LBO-able profile (stable cash flow, low capex, low leverage, asset-backed) plus price below LBO-floor multiple is a margin-of-safety signal. Also scenario toggles (base/downside: growth to ~2%, EBITDA margin -3 to -4 pts, capex +0.5pt of sales, inventory days +5) for bear-case fair value.
