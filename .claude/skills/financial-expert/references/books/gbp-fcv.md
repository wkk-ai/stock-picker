### gbp-fcv-aulas-5-6 (read fully)
- Risk/return: expected value of cash flow (example 118.5), portfolio expected return (33.1% weighted example), scenario expected return 17.4% and stdev 20.0%; correlation effect: corr 1 -> no diversification (stdev 18.57%); corr 0.67 -> pair stdev 7.55% with same 15% return. Systematic risk stays (~18% stdev) as number of stocks grows to 1,000s; only systematic risk priced.
- Beta = Cov(ri, rM)/Var(rM), slope of regression vs Ibovespa/S&P 500. BR betas on 09/04/2015 for listed examples ranged 0.57 to 1.46 (e.g. 0.74, 0.95, 0.57, 1.43, 1.46, 1.06). Caveat repeated: low liquidity stocks show low beta without lower risk; beta changes with window.
- CAPM: ri = rf + (rM - rf) x beta. BR example: LTN 10% a.a., Ibovespa average return 15%, beta 1.23 -> 16.2%. SML slide: LTN 11% a.a. as rf; Ibovespa 1998-2008 return 20.44% a.a. as market return (so market premium ~9.4pp using nominal BRL history) - period-dependent, large sensitivity. Project selection: compare return vs SML at the project's own beta (A 15%/beta 0.4 accepted; B 25%/beta 1.8 rejected vs firm 22%).
- Kd after tax = Kd x (1 - T), T = 34% in BR; methods: average cost of current debt (backward-looking) or YTM of similar-rated bonds (promised yield > expected return). WACC = Ke E/(D+E) + Kd(1-T) D/(D+E) at market weights.
- Capital structure: risk conservation (business risk constant; equity beta rises with debt); advantages: tax shield, fixed claim, control; disadvantages: Ke up, financing cost up, bankruptcy costs (suppliers shorten terms, customers leave, lenders tighten, owners withhold capital). Static trade-off (Myers 84): optimum where marginal tax benefit = marginal distress cost.
- Levered beta: beta_l = beta_u x (1 + D/E x (1-T)); use comps unlevered, relever at target D/E; compare betas only on same leverage.
- ROE = NI/PL; DuPont ROE = margem líquida x giro do ativo x alavancagem (Ativo/PL).
### gbp-fcv-aulas-7-8 (read fully)
- EVA = NOPAT - WACC x capital operacional (Stern Stewart). Wmellon 2011: NOPAT 796k, IC 4,010k, WACC 18% -> EVA +74k even though FCF -514k; projected EVA path 74, 260, 389, 1,846, 4,112, 4,743 (k) to 2016E. Levers: invest where return > WACC, improve return on existing capital, redeploy capital from sub-WACC projects, manage D/E to reduce WACC without excess risk.
- MVA = market value of firm - invested capital = PV of expected EVAs; "growth does not create value, growth with excess returns does".
- DCF steps: project DRE, BP, DFC -> FCF; Ke (CAPM), Kd, target D/E; WACC; terminal value. Macro drivers to project: interest rate (Selic), inflation, GDP growth, FX, seasonality, competition. BP projected via historical days of inventory/payables ratios.
- Terminal value: TV = FCFn(1+g)/(Ko-g), Ko typically WACC. Share of TV in value (r 12%, FCF growth 10%): g=0%: 57/41/31/25% for 5/10/15/20 years; g=5%: 70/55/45/37%; g=10%: 90/82/75/68%. Horizon should be long enough for stabilization; TV must not depend on horizon length; TV for firms far above the <10% typical of projects.
- AFP survey (HBR Jul-Aug 2012) of practice: horizon mostly 5 yrs (46%) or 10 yrs (34%); rf: 10-yr (46%), 30-yr, shorter; ERP: 5-6% (49%), 3-4% (23%), 7%+ (17%); beta window: 5 yrs 41%, 3 yrs 15%, 1-2 yrs; D/E target book vs market mixed; Kd: current debt rate 37%, historical average 34%, forecast future rate 29%. Takeaway: assumptions vary widely.
- Multiples: comps funnel (universe -> narrow by cost structure -> restrict by operation, e.g. airline TAM peers in the Americas). Adjust for LIFO/FIFO, extraordinaries, non-recurring (asset sales), owner compensation, acquired intangibles, non-operating assets, ongoing investments, tax incentives. EV = market cap + debt - cash & equivalents. EV/{EBIT, EBITA, EBITDA, sales, gross margin, total assets}; market cap/{NI, dividends, cash flow, EBT, book equity}.
- EV intuition: buying equity you also assume debt (price = mktcap + debt) and acquire cash (net price = mktcap - cash).
- P/E sector example (Dec 2014, ResiX NI R$550M projected 2015): peer P/Es 8.24, 13.08, 15.0, 5.17, 8.50; mean 10.0, weighted 9.46, max 15.0, min 5.17 => implied value R$2.84bn to 8.25bn; choice of multiple depends on industry features.
- Good practice: (1) EV/EBITA is usually best; P/E distorted by capital structure and non-operating results; (2) numerator/denominator consistent (if cash excluded from EV, exclude interest income from earnings); (3) peers with similar ROIC and growth.
- Examples: P/E varies 16.7x vs 12.5x for same EV/EBITA 10x (leverage); 41.7x vs 44.4x at EV/EBITA 25x; EV/EBIT 8.6x vs 7.5x for same business after acquired intangible amortization; EV/EBITDA 6.0x vs 8.6x for firms with same EBITA and FCF 150 but different capex/outsourcing (own plants D&A 200 vs outsourced 50).

## Book synthesis
1. Core valuation formula set to hard-code and expose in app: FCFF = EBIT(1-t)+D&A-capex-dNWC; ROIC = NOPAT/(NWC+operating fixed assets); EVA = (ROIC-WACC) x IC; WACC at market weights; Ke = rf + beta x ERP; Kd after tax with Brazil 34% statutory rate.
2. Rank cheapness primarily by EV/EBITA (or EV/EBIT with acquired-intangible amortization added back); show P/E, EV/EBITDA and P/B as secondary because each is distorted (leverage, capex intensity, acquisition accounting). Always keep numerator/denominator consistent (EV vs equity; strip financial income when cash is netted from EV).
3. Brazil rf/ERP choice must be explicit and BRL-consistent: course uses LTN yield as rf and historical Ibovespa return as market return (e.g. 20.4% a.a. 1998-2008, a high-inflation era), giving unstable premia; app should rely on current Tesouro prefixado/NTN-B (IPCA+ real for real-rate DCF) and a configurable ERP (plus country risk premium if USD basis), with sensitivity bands.
4. Selic/inflation: treat rates, inflation, FX as the first projection drivers; with high Selic (14%+) WACC and discount rates rise, compress long-duration growth stocks; financial result swings net income so rank on operating metrics (EBITA, ROIC) rather than P/E alone.
5. Terminal value discipline: cap g near long-run inflation/nominal GDP, flag valuations where TV >75% of EV, and show WACC x g sensitivity grid; compare DCF with sector multiples as cross-check.
6. Beta handling: unlever with sector D/E, relever for target; flag illiquid B3 tickers (artificially low beta) and use sector/peer betas for them; typical 3-5 yr window (AFP survey) with Ibovespa as index; S&P 500 for US names.
7. Value-trap and risk flags: highly levered equity near default (risk-shifting incentive; distress costs), negative CCC reliant on supplier credit, growth with ROIC<WACC, profit with negative operating cash flow, EBITDA presented as cash. Quality: ROIC>WACC, stable CFO/NI, low CCC trend, DuPont decomposition to see if ROE is leverage-driven.
8. Peer-group logic: choose peers by operating similarity (cost structure, geography, ROIC, growth) not just CVM/GICS sector; normalize one-offs before multiples; show min/median/weighted-average/max like the ResiX example (range 5.2x-15x implies value spread 3x) rather than a single multiple.
9. Capital-structure sanity: compare D/E and net debt/EBITDA to sector; trade-off theory says optimal debt is interior; excess debt raises Ke and distress risk, zero debt may leave WACC higher.
10. For CVM/IFRS data ingestion: DFP/ITR statements give net revenue, EBIT, D&A, working-capital lines; build days metrics on 360-day convention with average balances, and keep cash vs financial investments separation (financial assets excluded from NWC, included in net debt).
